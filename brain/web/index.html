/01 One brain

Four sensors. One brain.

Gmail, Google Drive, the PC agent and the decoy links all send one event format to one brain. It scores every change, keeps the attack-path graph and decides what needs the owner and what it can handle alone.

/02 Chakravyuh

Every path, scored.

Chakravyuh maps how an attacker gets from a stolen password to Clinic money or Patient records. Risk is the sum, over every path, of how easy each step is times what's at the end. It then names the one fix that removes the most risk.

/03 Ophanim

Wheels of eyes.

The Ophanim are the wheels within wheels, covered in eyes, that never stop turning. Nothing about the clinic's accounts or PC changes without one of them seeing it, and a tamper-proof log records that it did.

/ Capabilities

Two parts. One watch.

Chakravyuh is the defence: sensors, brain, graph and console. Sahayak is the helper that talks to the owner and staff on Telegram.

Chakravyuh

The defence

Sees the attack, scores it, contains what must be contained, and shows the path on the console.

C/01 Attack-path graph

Risk score + one fix

A live graph from Attacker to Clinic money and Patient records. Each arrow has an ease; each path's risk is the ease product × the value at the end. Shows the single fix that closes the most paths.

Risk 12.454 open paths

C/02 Email rule watch

Hidden forwarding, caught

Snapshots Gmail filters, forwarding and POP/IMAP access every poll and diffs them. Scores each new rule: forwards outside +3, hides mail +2, money or security words +2, catch-all +1, recent change +2.

5+ alert3–4 ask<3 digest

C/03 Drive watch

Public links, made private

Notices a file shared as "anyone with the link". One tap makes it private; the evidence is saved first and the setting is re-read to prove the fix worked.

C/04 PC agent

Ransomware tripwire

Decoy files, write entropy and rename bursts; any two of three fire it. The PC contains itself with its own firewall (only the brain stays reachable), freezes the process writing most and can restore from backup.

Only automatic action

C/05 Honeytokens

Decoys that tell on intruders

A fake "Netbanking login" file and a decoy email carry links no real user should open. Opening one raises an alert; link-preview bots are ignored.

C/06 Correlation

One attack, one incident

Email and PC alerts about the same person within 24 hours merge into one incident, severity goes up, and the runbook comes out in the right order.

C/07 Tamper alarm

Silence is an alert

The agent checks in every 5 seconds. Fifteen seconds of silence, or someone stopping it, is treated as hostile and reported.

C/08 Audit log

Tamper-proof record

Every event, alert and tap is chained with SHA-256. The console shows verified, or the exact row where the chain broke.

C/09 Expert view

MITRE ATT&CK + report

For the security person in the room: each incident's ATT&CK techniques and a report with its full audit trail.

Sahayak

The helper

A Telegram bot that turns alerts into plain words and one-tap decisions for people who are not security experts.

S/01 Plain alerts

Three lines, no jargon

Owner and staff get different copies. A language model may reword the message, checked for safety, but never decides anything; it falls back to the template.

S/02 Signed buttons

One tap, signed

"Remove it", "Make private", "Yes, it's mine". Every button is HMAC-signed, works once and expires in 15 minutes; owner-only actions refuse staff.

S/03 Paisa Pause

Stop before money moves

A vendor's bank details changed or an urgent payment request? Sahayak pauses it and walks through checking with the vendor on a known number.

S/04 Ask Sahayak

Answers from your guides

Ask a question in plain words and get an answer drawn from the clinic's own safety guides.

S/05 Runbook

Step by step, with why

Each runbook step comes as a button with what to do and why. "Call my IT helper" sends the technical details to the IT contact.

S/06 Check this

Photos and links

Forward a suspicious screenshot or link and get a hint on whether it's safe.

/ The demo, end to end

One attack, one incident.

Attackers don't stay in one place. Ophanim follows the chain across email and the PC and hands the owner one incident with the steps in order.

  1. 01

    Entry

    Password stolen

    Priya's Gmail has no 2-step login. That arrow on the graph is the easiest way in.

  2. 02

    Email

    Hidden email rule

    A filter forwards invoice emails outside and skips the inbox. Scored 7: alert. The path turns red.

  3. 03

    PC

    Ransomware on the front desk

    The agent trips, isolates the PC with its own firewall and reports to the brain.

  4. 04

    Brain

    One merged incident

    "Priya's email and her computer are both under attack." Critical, one alert, not three.

  5. 05

    Owner

    Fixed in order

    Contain PC → save evidence → confirm → remove rules → change password from a clean device → sign out other devices → check payments → report. All paths go grey.
